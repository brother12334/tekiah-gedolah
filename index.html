    Our Story

    The name is
    the whole joke

    Teki'ah gedolah — תקיעה גדולה — is the last blast of the shofar: one note, held as long as the breath lasts, the sound the whole service has been building toward. Say it out loud and you'll hear the other word hiding in it.

    So the ram's horn goes on the label, the blessing goes on the box, and the line underneath writes itself: it will blow you away.

    Behind the joke is a serious bottle. Teki'ah Gedolah is produced under the El Poderío mark in Tequila, Jalisco, distilled from 100% blue agave, rested in oak and then filtered clear — a cristalino that keeps the rested character and loses the colour. Kosher certified, bottled at 80 proof, each bottle numbered and signed by hand.

    The Nose

    Clean, and
    very green

    Cooked agave first — sweet, vegetal, unmistakable — then citrus peel and a little white pepper. The oak sits underneath rather than on top, which is the whole point of a cristalino.

    Find a bottle 80 proof
    The Palate

    Rested, then
    filtered clear

    Time in oak gives it vanilla and a soft, round middle; the charcoal filtering takes the colour back out. What's left is a reposado that drinks like a blanco and finishes like something older.

    The Finish

    Held to
    the last

    Warm, dry and long, with roasted agave and a trace of oak smoke well after the glass is down. Given what the bottle is named after, holding the last note is more or less the job.

    The Ritual

    Four ways to pour it

    Honey and pomegranate run through most of these — the two sweetnesses the new year already asks for. However you serve it, say the blessing first. The tequila will wait.

    01

    Neat, in a glass

    Room temperature, no ice, a wide-mouthed glass. A cristalino has nowhere to hide, which is exactly why it's worth drinking this way first.

    2 oz Teki'ah Gedolah
    02

    The Tishrei

    2 oz Teki'ah Gedolah, ¾ oz fresh pomegranate juice, ½ oz lemon, a bar spoon of honey syrup. Shake hard, strain over one large cube, float three pomegranate seeds.

    • Honey
    • Pomegranate
    Our house cocktail
    03

    Apples & Honey

    2 oz Teki'ah Gedolah stirred with ½ oz spiced apple cordial and a dash of orange bitters. Serve up, with a thin slice of honeycrisp across the rim.

    • Honey
    For the new year
    04

    Hava TeKI'lah

    Nobody dances the hora alone, so this one comes in a pitcher.

    Warm ½ cup wildflower honey with ½ cup water and a strip of orange peel until it loosens, then stir in ¾ cup pomegranate juice and let it cool — that's your honey-pomegranate syrup, and it keeps a week.

    In a pitcher over plenty of ice: 12 oz Teki'ah Gedolah, 5 oz of the syrup, 3 oz fresh lime. Stir, then top with 10 oz cold soda water. Garnish with pomegranate seeds and a slapped sprig of mint, and pour for whoever is nearest.

    • Wildflower honey
    • Pomegranate
    Serves six · הבה נגילה
